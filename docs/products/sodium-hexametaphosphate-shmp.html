Sodium Hexametaphosphate SHMP

A condensed phosphate commonly assessed for sequestration, dispersion and mineral-management tasks.

INGREDIENT OVERVIEW

SHMP: product profile

Commercial SHMP is described in JECFA as sodium polyphosphate, glassy. Specify the commercial composition rather than assuming a pure, fixed ring molecule from the name.

Also known as: SHMP · Sodium polyphosphate, glassy · Graham's salt

SHMP is relevant when mineral interactions, dispersion or a phosphate system are part of the formulation problem. In a beverage, clarity and sediment may matter most; in a protein food, processing and texture introduce a different set of observations.

GRADE & PROCESS

Mineral management with a commercial polyphosphate

Choosing the material

Commercial SHMP is a glassy polyphosphate material. Its name does not define one pure ring compound or one chain-length distribution. Total phosphate, inactive phosphate and solution behavior describe different aspects of a grade.

Planning a useful comparison

Compare the actual process water, mineral load, pH and heating sequence. Record solution preparation, initial clarity and changes after the intended hold or storage period rather than judging only the freshly mixed sample.

Describe the system: Describe the food matrix, water/mineral conditions, protein content, pH, heat history and the problem being observed.

PURCHASING QUESTIONS

SHMP: what to ask next.

Does the name identify one molecular composition?

Do not assume that it does. Request the source’s commercial identity and composition information. The published glassy-polyphosphate identity is a reference, not a specification for a particular offer.

What makes a pH comparison useful?

Use matching concentration, water, preparation and temperature, and note the method and document revision. An unqualified pH number cannot resolve a grade comparison.

When should I compare SHMP with STPP?

When both are candidates for the same stated problem. Test mineral interactions, dispersion or water-management objectives in the actual food rather than declaring either universally better.

Packaging, handling & ordering
