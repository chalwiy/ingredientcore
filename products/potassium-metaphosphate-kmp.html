PRODUCT OVERVIEW

What is potassium metaphosphate?

WHO/JECFA lists potassium metaphosphate, potassium polymetaphosphate and potassium polyphosphate together for CAS 7790-53-6. The corresponding FAO monograph describes a mixture of potassium salts of linear condensed polyphosphoric acids. These names have a defined reference context; they do not identify every potassium polyphosphate blend or establish one fixed chain length.

Names in the cited identity context: KMP · Potassium polymetaphosphate · Potassium polyphosphate

WHO/JECFA identity reference · FAO material definition and identification methods — these records describe the reference material, not an offered grade or destination-market approval.

COMPOSITION & PREPARATION

KMP composition and sample preparation

Grade identity and composition

Ask for the proposed food-grade material description, source, composition information and current specification. Match the commercial name to the CAS and record scope. A repeat-unit formula or phosphorus content alone does not establish the distribution of phosphate species or the preparation behavior of the proposed grade.

Preparation before measurement

The FAO identity monograph specifies particular media for its solubility and gel-formation tests. Those are identification procedures, not a food recipe or a general water-solubility guarantee. For your trial, obtain the source preparation guidance and record medium, concentration, temperature, mixing and elapsed time before measuring the prepared sample.

BUYER QUESTIONS

KMP purchasing questions

Is potassium polyphosphate always the same as KMP?

No. The cited WHO/JECFA record groups these names for a defined material identity. A commercial description may cover another composition or blend. Match the exact name, CAS, source description and specification before comparing products.

Which conditions make a viscosity result comparable?

Request the unit and complete method, including preparation medium, concentration, temperature, sample history, instrument, geometry or spindle, speed and reading time. Compare the same prepared state; a number without this context cannot define equivalent grades.

Can I assume the preparation behaves like SHMP or KTPP?

No. Match the actual phosphate identity, composition and preparation guidance, then test your medium and process. Neither the word metaphosphate nor the shared potassium component establishes equivalent behavior.

Can this replace a sodium phosphate at the same weight?

There is no general equal-weight substitution rule. Match the phosphate identity, grade, reporting basis and preparation conditions, then evaluate the complete food formulation.

What should I include in a sample request?

Include the food, process, evaluation objective and required documents. Free sample arrangements are discussed with sales; identify the sample grade and source before comparing it with a commercial offer.
