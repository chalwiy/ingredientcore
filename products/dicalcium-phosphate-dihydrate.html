PRODUCT OVERVIEW

What is dicalcium phosphate dihydrate?

Dicalcium phosphate dihydrate is calcium hydrogen phosphate with two waters of crystallization. It is a distinct hydration identity from anhydrous DCP; the shared abbreviation does not make the two materials identical.

Also known as: DCP dihydrate · Calcium hydrogen phosphate dihydrate · Dibasic calcium phosphate dihydrate · DCPD

Identity/background reference — material identity, not confirmation of an offered grade or destination-market approval.

HYDRATION & METHODS

DCP dihydrate water and thermal tests

Crystallization water is part of the identity

The dihydrate formula includes two waters of crystallization. That description is different from free moisture on a sample. Request a record that names the hydrate, and a method for any reported water result. A theoretical formula calculation cannot replace a sample’s measured result.

Drying loss and ignition loss need their own methods

Keep the procedure name, temperature sequence, duration, pretreatment and reporting basis with each thermal result. A measured mass loss can depend on the procedure and material changes; do not relabel ignition loss as free moisture or compare values obtained by different methods as if they were equivalent.

BUYER QUESTIONS

DCP dihydrate purchasing questions

Are crystallization water and free moisture the same?

No. Crystallization water belongs to the hydrate identity, while a free-moisture result requires a defined analytical procedure. Do not substitute a theoretical water fraction for a measured sample result.

Can an anhydrous-basis assay describe a dihydrate?

Yes, an analytical expression can be separate from the original physical form. Ask for the sample identity, pretreatment and calculation basis so that the result is interpreted correctly.

Can drying loss be compared directly with ignition loss?

Only compare results with their complete methods and intended measurands. Procedure names, temperature, duration and basis must remain visible in the source records; neither result automatically means free moisture.

Can DCPD replace anhydrous DCP at equal weight?

An equal-weight replacement cannot be assumed. Review the actual composition, particle data and complete food process, then design a comparison that keeps sample identities and analytical bases clear.

What should I send when requesting a sample?

Specify DCP dihydrate, the food, process and comparison objective. Request a form-specific specification, thermal methods, TDS, COA and SDS. Contact sales for free sample arrangements, a quotation and certificates for the applicable product, source, holder and scope.
