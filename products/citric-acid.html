MATERIAL OVERVIEW

What is citric acid?

Citric acid is an organic acid used in food acidity and flavor discussions. Anhydrous citric acid and citric acid monohydrate have different water-of-crystallization identities. A purchase brief should name the form rather than use “CA” alone.

This guide supports food manufacturers, ingredient importers and distributors comparing material requirements. IngredientCore is a food ingredient export subsidiary of Bespring Chemical serving global B2B markets; this material remains a selection reference.

Citric acid anhydrous

Formula: C₆H₈O₇
CAS: 77-92-9

Specify the anhydrous form on the specification and analytical report. Do not apply monohydrate moisture figures to it.

Citric acid monohydrate

Formula: C₆H₈O₇·H₂O
CAS: 5949-29-1

The formula includes one molecule of crystal water. Its identity and reporting basis must remain explicit when comparing material quantities.

Identity reference: FAO/JECFA citric acid monograph (1997). This historical reference identifies the two forms; it does not establish a proposed product’s conformity.

FOOD APPLICATIONS

Compare acidity in your food and process

pH describes hydrogen-ion activity; titratable acidity measures neutralization to a stated endpoint. Buffering describes resistance to pH change. Sourness is a sensory response in the complete food. Record these separately rather than treating one measurement as a prediction of all four.

Beverages

State the acid system, water composition, sugar and flavor profile, mixing and heat treatment. Compare pH, titratable acidity and sensory balance under the intended serving conditions.

Review beverage screeningDiscuss a beverage brief ↗

Sauces & prepared foods

Record the full formulation, process, filling and storage conditions. Observe acidity and texture separately; an acid addition alone is not a validated preservation process.

Review sauces and prepared foodsDiscuss a sauce brief ↗

Confectionery screening

Specify the desired sensory profile, incorporation stage and food matrix. Evaluate dispersion, appearance and taste in the actual product rather than transferring an aqueous comparison directly.

SELECTION QUESTIONS

Citric acid selection questions

Are anhydrous and monohydrate interchangeable by weight?

Do not assume equal-weight interchangeability. Name the form and assay basis, and have the formulation team review any change in the complete recipe.

Does matching pH mean matching sourness?

No. Record pH, titratable acidity and sensory observations separately. The food matrix and acid system are part of the comparison.

Are the moisture figures a supply specification?

No. They are form-separated attachment references. A proposed source’s current specification and test method are needed for acceptance limits.

What should I include in a document request?

Name the form, food application, destination, intended assessment and required records. If a source is proposed, request its specification, reporting bases and representative analytical report.
