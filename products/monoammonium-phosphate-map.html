PRODUCT OVERVIEW

What is monoammonium phosphate?

Monoammonium Phosphate is ammonium dihydrogen phosphate, an ammonium orthophosphate. Its identity formula is NH₄H₂PO₄. Diammonium phosphate (DAP) is a different ammonium salt; a family name does not establish the same composition, specification or replacement rule.

Also known as: MAP · Ammonium dihydrogen phosphate · Ammonium phosphate monobasic

Identity/background reference — material identity, not confirmation of an offered grade or destination-market approval.

BUYER QUESTIONS

MAP purchasing questions

What does MAP mean on this page?

MAP means monoammonium phosphate, also called ammonium dihydrogen phosphate. Use the full name and food-grade requirement in your inquiry so the abbreviation does not stand alone.

Does P₂O₅ content describe MAP purity?

No. P₂O₅ is a phosphorus reporting expression; MAP assay reports a different quantity. Ask for the analyte, units, method and dry or as-is basis rather than comparing the percentages as if they were interchangeable.

Should I specify powder or granules?

Describe your handling and incorporation route, and request the actual form, particle information and declared composition for the proposed grade. We do not list a confirmed range of physical grades here.

Can MAP replace DAP at the same weight?

The names identify different salts. Define the nutrient and acid–base objectives, compare source records and evaluate the complete food process before accepting any substitution.

How can I request MAP samples and purchasing records?

Send the intended food, evaluation purpose, target specification, quantity and destination. Request the specification, TDS, COA, SDS and required certificates. Free sample arrangements are discussed with sales; match the trial grade and source to the material proposed for purchase.
