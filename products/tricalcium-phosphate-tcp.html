PRODUCT OVERVIEW

What is tricalcium phosphate?

Tricalcium phosphate is also called tribasic calcium phosphate. The JECFA food-additive monograph describes a commercial mixture of calcium phosphates and expresses assay as an equivalent of Ca₃(PO₄)₂ on an ignited basis. This is a food-additive identity and analytical convention, not proof that every offered product is a single pure crystalline phase. Request the actual grade description rather than inferring a crystal form or particle size from the name.

Also known as: TCP · Tribasic calcium phosphate · Calcium phosphate tribasic · Tricalcium orthophosphate

Identity/background reference — material identity, not confirmation of an offered grade or destination-market approval.

GRADE & FUNCTION

Choose TCP for your food and process

Mineral reporting and compound-equivalent assay

Keep measured calcium as Ca and phosphorus expressed as P₂O₅ separate from a compound-equivalent assay. Request the analytical method, pretreatment and calculation basis for each. The Ca₃(PO₄)₂ expression does not establish the actual phase composition, and a theoretical calcium calculation cannot replace a source analysis.

Particle data and application records

Request the proposed grade’s particle distribution and method, together with handling or dispersion data relevant to your process. A grade that works in a dry powder still needs a separate evaluation in a liquid food. Food-use records must identify the actual source; pharmaceutical tableting data or a competitor’s specialized mineral grade do not establish the proposed product’s behavior.

TCP in dry blends and dispersed foods

Seasonings and other dry food blends

TCP is listed as an anticaking agent in the linked JECFA monograph. Define the blend, particle requirements, mixing, transfer route, humidity exposure, package and storage period. Compare flow, caking and segregation against your control using a consistent observation method. The general function does not guarantee anticaking performance in every powder.

Dispersed mineral formulations

For a food mineral formulation, provide the target composition, liquid matrix, pH, mixing and heat treatment. Observe sediment, redispersion, texture and appearance at defined times. The JECFA material is practically insoluble in water: an acid-solubility result is not evidence of water dissolution. Assess nutrition and labeling requirements separately from these processing observations.

BUYER QUESTIONS

TCP purchasing questions

Does TCP mean a single pure crystalline phase?

Not necessarily. The cited JECFA food-additive definition describes a variable mixture of calcium phosphates and uses a compound-equivalent assay expression. Request the actual grade’s material description; the product name does not specify a phase or particle size.

Is calcium content the same as TCP assay?

No. Ca is a mineral-reporting expression, P₂O₅ is a different expression, and a compound-equivalent assay requires its own definition and basis. Do not infer purity or nutritional benefit from a theoretical composition.

Does a dry-flow result establish liquid stability?

No. Dry powder handling and liquid dispersion require different trials. The cited identity monograph describes the material as practically insoluble in water; evaluate sediment and redispersion in the complete liquid food.

What should a TCP sample request include?

Name the food and whether the objective is powder handling or mineral-formulation evaluation. Include the process, target specification and observations. Request particle and analytical records, TDS, COA and SDS; contact sales for free samples, a quotation and customer-required certificates for the applicable product and source.
