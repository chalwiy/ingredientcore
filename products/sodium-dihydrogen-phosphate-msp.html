PRODUCT OVERVIEW

What is monosodium phosphate?

Sodium dihydrogen phosphate, or monosodium phosphate, is a sodium orthophosphate. Its formula is NaH₂PO₄ for the anhydrous material; monohydrate is NaH₂PO₄·H₂O and dihydrate is NaH₂PO₄·2H₂O. These identity distinctions do not establish availability of every form.

Also known as: MSP · Monosodium phosphate · Sodium phosphate monobasic · Sodium dihydrogen orthophosphate

Identity/background reference — material identity, not confirmation of an offered grade or destination-market approval.

GRADE & FORM

Specify the MSP hydration form

Specify the formula with its water

Use the same hydration designation on the specification, sample label, COA and quotation. A monohydrate formula includes one water of crystallization; a dihydrate includes two. The assay basis is a separate statement and should not be used to infer the physical form.

Describe the complete buffer system

Give the acids, DSP or other salts, minerals and target pH behavior. Compare the food after mixing and processing rather than selecting MSP from a raw-material pH number alone.

BUYER QUESTIONS

MSP purchasing questions

Is NaH₂PO₄ content the same as phosphorus or P₂O₅?

No. These are different reporting expressions. Ask which analyte is measured, how the result is calculated and whether it refers to the dried sample, the received material or another stated basis. Keep the original expression when comparing specifications.

Is monobasic sodium phosphate the same name as MSP?

It is a common name for sodium dihydrogen phosphate. The name still needs the hydration form, grade and source for purchasing.

Does an anhydrous reporting basis prove an anhydrous product?

No. The assay basis and the physical form are separate statements. Confirm both on the current source documents.

Can MSP replace DSP by matching final pH?

A single pH endpoint does not establish equivalent salt composition or buffer behavior. Review the complete recipe, form and process with the formulator.
