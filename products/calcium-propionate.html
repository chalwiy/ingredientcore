PRODUCT OVERVIEW

What is calcium propionate?

Calcium propionate, also known as calcium propanoate, is the calcium salt of propionic acid. Bakery manufacturers review the specified food-grade material alongside their recipe and preservation study. A material specification identifies what is purchased; it does not establish mold control or shelf life in the finished food.

Also known as: Calcium propanoate

JECFA identity reference — material identity, not confirmation of an offered grade or destination-market approval.

BUYER QUESTIONS

Calcium propionate purchasing questions

Does a calcium propionate COA prove bread shelf life?

No. A COA reports results for an identified material sample or lot. Finished-food preservation needs its own evaluation in the recipe, package and storage conditions.

What should I include in a bakery sample request?

Include the food, current preservative system, pH and water activity if measured, process, package, destination and evaluation objective. Sales can discuss the sample grade and shipping arrangements.

Can I exchange calcium and sodium propionate at the same weight?

Do not assume equivalence. Review the exact salts, assay basis, calcium/sodium contribution and food process with the formulator, then compare the named grades.

Is the dry-basis assay the same as propionic acid content?

No. The selected field names calcium propionate and states a dry basis. Request the assay method and reporting calculation; do not relabel the result as propionic acid content or compare it with another reporting basis without review.

Can the 10% solution pH predict my bread pH?

No. That measurement characterizes a prepared ingredient solution. Confirm its concentration definition and temperature, and measure the finished food separately as part of the evaluation.
