PRODUCT OVERVIEW

What is tetrasodium pyrophosphate?

Tetrasodium pyrophosphate (TSPP), also called tetrasodium diphosphate, is a phosphate reviewed for buffering, emulsification and sequestration in food systems. It is distinct from acidic SAPP. For procurement, identify the hydration form before comparing chemical content, water-loss results or solution pH.

Also known as: TSPP · Tetrasodium diphosphate · Sodium pyrophosphate

JECFA identity reference — material identity, not confirmation of an offered grade or destination-market approval.

BUYER QUESTIONS

TSPP purchasing questions

Is TSPP another name for SAPP?

No. Tetrasodium and disodium acid pyrophosphate have different identities and acid–base roles. Do not substitute by the shared pyrophosphate name.

Can the anhydrous table qualify a decahydrate?

No. Confirm the offered form and obtain its own specification, assay basis and water-loss methods. The presence of a hydrate in an identity reference is not a supply claim.

Does 10 g/L equal every specification described as 1%?

Not without the concentration definition and preparation method. Ask whether a percentage is mass/volume or mass/mass, and match water and temperature for the comparison.

Why are TSPP content and P₂O₅ listed separately?

The first field reports the named compound; the second expresses phosphate as a P₂O₅ equivalent. Compare the analyte, reporting basis and method before interpreting a percentage or reviewing a COA.

Does the same loss limit make drying and ignition interchangeable?

No. The temperature sequence, duration and sample form belong to the method. Request both procedures where applicable; the supplied excerpt does not define them.
