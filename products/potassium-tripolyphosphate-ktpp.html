PRODUCT OVERVIEW

What is potassium tripolyphosphate?

Potassium tripolyphosphate is pentapotassium triphosphate, a condensed phosphate with three phosphorus atoms in its phosphate unit. It is distinct from TKPP and potassium metaphosphate. STPP is the sodium tripolyphosphate; the related chemical names do not establish identical composition, preparation or food performance.

Also known as: KTPP · Pentapotassium triphosphate · Pentapotassium tripolyphosphate · Potassium triphosphate

Identity/background reference — material identity, not confirmation of an offered grade or destination-market approval.

BUYER QUESTIONS

KTPP purchasing questions

Does a P₂O₅ result show the triphosphate percentage?

No. The phosphorus reporting convention and triphosphate assay answer different questions. Request both with their methods and reporting bases when they are needed for qualification.

Does water-insoluble matter tell me how fast KTPP dissolves?

No. A residue test and a preparation-time observation measure different things. Ask for the test definition and evaluate the proposed material in your actual water, temperature and mixing conditions.

Can KTPP replace STPP at the same weight?

There is no general equal-weight substitution rule. Match the phosphate identity, grade, reporting basis and preparation conditions, then evaluate the complete food formulation.

What should I include in a sample request?

Include the food, process, evaluation objective and required documents. Free sample arrangements are discussed with sales; identify the sample grade and source before comparing it with a commercial offer.
