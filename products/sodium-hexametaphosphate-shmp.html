PRODUCT OVERVIEW

What is sodium hexametaphosphate?

Sodium hexametaphosphate (SHMP) is a commercial glassy sodium polyphosphate used for sequestration, emulsification and texture-related functions in food systems. Buyers consider it for beverages, meat, seafood and dairy applications, then evaluate the proposed grade in their own formulation and process.

Common names: Sodium Hexametaphosphate (SHMP), Sodium polyphosphate, glassy, and Graham’s salt.

The JECFA identity reference describes a family of materials rather than one pure six-unit ring. For purchasing, specify the food application, required physical form, target specification and destination market.

JECFA identity reference — material identity, not confirmation of an offered grade or destination-market approval.

Where SHMP fits in food processing

Beverages

Start with the sediment, clarity or dispersion issue. Record water hardness or mineral inputs, pH, mixing and heat treatment. During a sample trial, compare dissolution, visible sediment and the finished drink under the intended storage conditions.

Review the food application

Meat and seafood

Describe salt, brine preparation, contact, draining and heat or freeze history. Keep treatment uptake separate from retained water after processing.

Review the food application

Dairy systems

For a dairy or processed-cheese review, identify the protein base, mineral ingredients, other phosphates and heating sequence. Compare dispersion and finished texture in the complete system, rather than assessing SHMP in water alone.

Review the food application

The JECFA monograph identifies emulsifier, sequestrant and texturizer functions. A sequestrant interacts with mineral ions; this functional identity does not establish performance in a particular food.

These are evaluation questions. Review the exact food category, use conditions and labeling for the destination market separately from sample performance.

BUYER QUESTIONS

SHMP purchasing questions

Is SHMP a single pure hexamer?

The commercial glassy polyphosphate identity does not establish a single ring structure or chain distribution. Match the source’s identity and composition records to the proposed grade.

Can I compare an SHMP P₂O₅ result with STPP assay?

Not directly. First identify the analyte, units, basis and analytical method. A compound assay and a phosphorus-equivalent result express different things.

What should accompany a sample request?

Include the food, water or mineral conditions, observed problem and process. Ask sales to identify the sample grade and source so that trial results can be linked to the proposed order.

Why does the specification need pH test conditions?

A pH range is useful only with the concentration, water, preparation and measurement temperature. The supplied excerpt does not include these conditions; ask for the current source method before comparing results.

Which records connect a free sample with a commercial order?

Ask for the sample identity, grade and source, a corresponding specification and a sample or representative COA. After evaluation, confirm that the proposed commercial material is covered by the agreed documents.
