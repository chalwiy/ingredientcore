MATERIAL OVERVIEW

What is trisodium citrate?

Trisodium citrate is the tribasic sodium salt of citric acid. Its name identifies a particular salt; it should not stand in for every sodium citrate material or an unspecified blend.

For a trisodium citrate review, keep the salt and hydrate in the same identity record. Anhydrous and dihydrate formulas are shown below as material references, not as grades offered by IngredientCore.

Trisodium citrate anhydrous

Reference formula: C₆H₅Na₃O₇.

The identity excludes crystal water. Its assay and drying criteria should be reviewed against the corresponding form.

Trisodium citrate dihydrate

Reference formula: C₆H₅Na₃O₇·2H₂O.

The identity includes two molecules of crystal water. This form must be named in the proposed specification and formula calculations.

Identity and general functional classes: FAO/JECFA trisodium citrate monograph. The reference lists anhydrous and hydrated forms and buffer, sequestrant and emulsion-stabilizer functions. It is not evidence of a source’s grade, conformity or finished-food performance.

FOOD APPLICATIONS

Assess buffering in the complete food

Buffering means resistance to pH change. It is not the same as a single pH reading, titratable acidity or sensory sourness. Define the acid–salt composition, concentration and complete food matrix for a comparison.

Beverages & acid systems

Describe the acids already present, target pH, titratable-acidity method, water minerals and sodium contribution. Observe taste, clarity and pH separately during processing and storage.

SELECTION QUESTIONS

Sodium citrate selection questions

Does “sodium citrate” identify a specific hydrate?

No. Use the full salt and hydrate name in the purchase brief, then check the source’s formula and identity records. This guide discusses trisodium citrate forms without confirming a proposed source’s hydrate.

Can a CAS number alone settle the hydration question?

Do not rely on an isolated identifier. Review the CAS, full name, formula, hydrate and specification together and resolve any discrepancy with the source.

Is a solution pH result a buffering specification?

No. A material test at a stated concentration is a specific analytical result. Buffering requires a defined acid–salt system and comparison method in the relevant matrix.

Can sodium citrate replace citric acid by equal weight?

No equal-weight replacement is recommended. The acid and sodium salt have different identities; define the intended pH, sensory or mineral objective and review the complete formula.

What is needed for a purchasing-support inquiry?

Name the salt and hydrate if already defined, food, destination, analytical requirements and requested records. Sales first confirms whether purchasing support and an appropriate source are available.
