PRODUCT OVERVIEW

What is tetrapotassium pyrophosphate?

Tetrapotassium pyrophosphate, also called tetrapotassium diphosphate, is a condensed phosphate with two phosphorus atoms in its phosphate unit. JECFA lists emulsifier and texturizer functions for this material. It is distinct from TKP, an orthophosphate, and KTPP, a tripolyphosphate; similar initials do not establish comparable food behavior.

Also known as: TKPP · Tetrapotassium diphosphate · Potassium pyrophosphate

Identity/background reference — material identity, not confirmation of an offered grade or destination-market approval.

BUYER QUESTIONS

TKPP purchasing questions

Why specify TKPP rather than potassium phosphate?

Potassium phosphate is a broad description. The pyrophosphate identity and formula separate TKPP from MKP, DKP, TKP and KTPP.

Which assay and thermal-loss details should I request?

Ask whether TKPP content is reported as-is, on a dry basis or on an ignited basis, and obtain the corresponding method. For a thermal-loss result, include the test name, temperature sequence and duration; the number alone cannot identify the test.

Can this replace a sodium phosphate at the same weight?

There is no general equal-weight substitution rule. Match the phosphate identity, grade, reporting basis and preparation conditions, then evaluate the complete food formulation.

Does a successful blend trial prove the effect of TKPP alone?

No. Keep the full salt system and process record with the result. A controlled comparison is needed to distinguish the contribution of a particular ingredient from the behavior of the blend.

What should I include in a sample request?

Include the food, process, evaluation objective and required documents. Free sample arrangements are discussed with sales; identify the sample grade and source before comparing it with a commercial offer.
