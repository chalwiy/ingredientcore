PRODUCT OVERVIEW

What is carrageenan?

Carrageenan is a family of polysaccharides obtained from red seaweed. Kappa and iota are gelling types under suitable conditions; lambda is a thickening type. These technical categories do not confirm our supply of every type.

For dairy, desserts and prepared foods, define whether the target is gel formation, texture or stabilization. Connect the selected material to its declared composition and your heating, cooling and salt system.

Also known as: Carrageenan gum

TYPE & COMPOSITION

Carrageenan type and refinement are different

Distinguish type from refinement

Kappa (κ) is commonly associated with firmer gels in potassium-containing systems; iota (ι) with more elastic gels in calcium-containing systems; lambda (λ) with thickening without its own gel formation. Refined and semi-refined describe processing. Neither term alone identifies the functional type or predicts texture in a food.

Confirm the proposed source and composition

Request the declared type, refinement or process identity, and whether the offered product is a single material, a standardized product or a blend. Ask for any added salts, sugars or other components relevant to your formulation. Sales can match the requirement to a documented product and source.

BUYER QUESTIONS

Carrageenan purchasing questions

Is food-grade carrageenan the same as poligeenan?

No. Poligeenan is a degraded material produced through extensive hydrolysis; it is not a synonym for food-grade carrageenan. Keep the identity and conditions of materials in research separate from the proposed food ingredient. The 2018 EFSA scientific opinion, sections 3.1.1 and 3.1.2, discusses this distinction; it does not verify an offered grade or establish permission for your food and market.

Are refined and kappa equivalent grade descriptions?

No. Refinement/process identity and functional type are separate dimensions. Ask for both and for any blend composition.

Which identity details should I include in a request?

Include any required type, refinement or process identity, composition and supplier grade code from your target specification. Ask for matching identity information on the current specification and SDS. The technical type descriptions above are not a list of confirmed available grades.

Why can two gel-strength numbers be misleading?

Different concentration, salts, heating/cooling, instrument, rate or temperature can describe different tests. Review full methods before comparing the grades.
