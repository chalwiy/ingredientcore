PRODUCT OVERVIEW

What is anhydrous dicalcium phosphate?

Anhydrous dicalcium phosphate is calcium hydrogen phosphate without water of crystallization. The DCP abbreviation alone does not establish the anhydrous form. Keep source records separate from those for DCP dihydrate. An assay calculated on an anhydrous basis does not by itself establish that a supplied sample is physically anhydrous.

Also known as: DCP anhydrous · Calcium hydrogen phosphate anhydrous · Dibasic calcium phosphate anhydrous

Identity/background reference — material identity, not confirmation of an offered grade or destination-market approval.

GRADE & FORM

Anhydrous DCP identity and assay basis

Verify the anhydrous material

Request a specification and SDS explicitly naming anhydrous calcium hydrogen phosphate, with the actual source and sample identifier. Food, feed, pharmaceutical and co-processed grades must not be treated as interchangeable because they share a chemical name. A form-specific file should describe the proposed food ingredient.

Read the assay definition first

A result expressed as CaHPO₄ on an anhydrous basis describes an analytical expression. It does not by itself prove the original sample had no crystallization water. Request the pretreatment, measured quantity and calculation basis; keep elemental Ca, elemental P and P₂O₅ separate from the compound assay.

BUYER QUESTIONS

Anhydrous DCP purchasing questions

Does DCP always mean the anhydrous form?

No. Dicalcium phosphate and calcium hydrogen phosphate can be used without a hydration qualifier. This page reviews the anhydrous identity; request a named form in the actual source records.

Does an anhydrous-basis assay identify the original sample?

No. The analytical basis and the sample’s hydration identity are separate. Ask for the material name, pretreatment and calculation method together.

Can I compare Ca, P₂O₅ and CaHPO₄ percentages directly?

They describe different reporting expressions. Match the measured quantity, sample basis, units and method rather than treating a mineral percentage as compound purity.

Which data help with a dispersion trial?

Request particle information and the test definition for any solubility or pH result. Share the food, mixing route, heat cycle and intended observations. Free samples, specifications, TDS, COA, SDS and customer-required certificates can be discussed with sales for the proposed source.
