PRODUCT OVERVIEW

What is sodium acid pyrophosphate?

Sodium acid pyrophosphate (SAPP), also called disodium pyrophosphate, is an acidic diphosphate used with sodium bicarbonate in chemical leavening systems. Bakery manufacturers and dry-mix buyers need both a defined chemical specification and a reaction profile suited to their mixing, holding and baking schedule.

Also known as: SAPP · Disodium pyrophosphate · Disodium dihydrogen diphosphate · Acid sodium pyrophosphate

JECFA identity reference — material identity, not confirmation of an offered grade or destination-market approval.

GRADE & REACTION

Chemical content, acid capacity and reaction timing

Ask for a defined reaction grade

A chemical assay does not establish the gas-release profile. Request the grade designation, reaction-test method and conditions relevant to your production schedule.

Separate balance from timing

Neutralizing value concerns acid capacity against bicarbonate; reaction behavior concerns gas-release timing. Request the definition and method for each rather than using a rate code as an acid-balance value.

BUYER QUESTIONS

SAPP purchasing questions

Does an SAPP grade number identify a universal reaction rate?

No. Ask how the supplier defines and measures it, and connect that definition to the sampled grade. Numbers from different procedures are not automatically comparable.

Can SAPP and SALP be exchanged at the same weight?

The material name alone is not a substitution basis. Have the formulator review acid capacity, bicarbonate balance, reaction timing and the exact acidic SALP grade before trials.

What if my target specification contains only assay and pH?

Those fields help characterize the chemical material, but do not define bakery timing. Add the process schedule and request any available reaction-grade records.

Does the pH range tell me how fast SAPP reacts in batter?

No. The reported pH is for a 1% aqueous solution and is separate from a reaction test in a defined leavening system. Request the proposed grade’s reaction procedure and relate it to your process.

What information helps sales arrange a useful sample?

Provide the food type, current leavening system, mixing-to-bake interval and any cold holding, together with the target specification and destination. Ask sales to identify the sample grade and source before evaluation.
