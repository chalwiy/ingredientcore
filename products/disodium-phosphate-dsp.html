PRODUCT OVERVIEW

What is disodium phosphate?

Disodium phosphate is disodium hydrogen orthophosphate, also called dibasic sodium phosphate. The anhydrous formula is Na₂HPO₄; hydrated materials contain crystallization water and need their own form declaration.

Also known as: DSP · Disodium hydrogen phosphate · Sodium phosphate dibasic · Dibasic sodium phosphate

Identity/background reference — material identity, not confirmation of an offered grade or destination-market approval.

BUYER QUESTIONS

DSP purchasing questions

What does “as anhydrous Na₂HPO₄” mean on an assay?

It states how the result is expressed. Ask for the calculation and sample-preparation basis as well as the delivered form. A hydrate can have an assay expressed as anhydrous salt; the label and formula are still needed to identify what is being purchased.

Are DSP and TSPP two names for the same material?

No. DSP is an orthophosphate; TSPP is a condensed pyrophosphate. Identify the exact salt before comparing an emulsifying-salt system.

Can one drying value apply to all DSP hydrates?

No. The form and procedure must match the specification. Ask for temperature, time, basis and the explicit acceptance limit.

What belongs in a dairy sample request?

Include protein and mineral inputs, fat/water, the existing salt system, processing and the texture or separation objective. Request the same hydrate and grade proposed for supply.
