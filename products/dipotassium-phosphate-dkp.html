PRODUCT OVERVIEW

What is dipotassium phosphate?

Dipotassium phosphate is dipotassium hydrogen phosphate, a dibasic potassium orthophosphate. Compound assay and a phosphorus result expressed as P₂O₅ describe different quantities.

Also known as: DKP · Dipotassium hydrogen phosphate · Dibasic potassium phosphate · Potassium phosphate dibasic

Identity/background reference — material identity, not confirmation of an offered grade or destination-market approval.

BUYER QUESTIONS

DKP purchasing questions

Is P₂O₅ content the same as DKP purity?

No. A result expressed as P₂O₅ is a phosphorus reporting convention. It cannot be read as the percentage of K₂HPO₄ in the product.

Can I compare pH values without solution conditions?

No. A comparable pH report needs the concentration and its basis, preparation water, temperature and measurement method. A material’s solution pH also differs from the pH achieved in a complete creamer or dairy recipe.

Does a dry-basis assay identify an anhydrous shipment?

No. The analytical basis and the physical hydration state answer different questions. Request the form-specific identity, assay procedure and sample record instead of selecting a hydrate from the percentage alone.

Can I replace a sodium phosphate at the same weight?

No general equal-weight substitution is established. Compare phosphate identity, material form, reporting basis and behavior in the complete food process.

How do I request a sample or quotation?

Share the product, food application, target specification, quantity and destination. Free sample arrangements are discussed with sales; request the documents needed for your evaluation.
