PRODUCT OVERVIEW

What is sodium propionate?

Sodium propionate, also known as sodium propanoate, is the sodium salt of propionic acid. This page covers the named salt, not propionic acid or a proprietary preservation blend. Food manufacturers should review the proposed source, chemical specification and incorporation route before evaluating it in a finished bakery product.

Also known as: Sodium propanoate

JECFA identity reference — material identity, not confirmation of an offered grade or destination-market approval.

SALT & SELECTION

Choose the salt on your recipe requirements

Explain the reason for the sodium salt

Share the existing material, recipe sodium/calcium constraints and the reason for considering sodium propionate. Food pH, handling and sensory acceptance belong in the comparison.

Use the stated solution conditions

The supplied pH field refers to a 10% aqueous solution at 20°C. It is not the pH of your finished food; request the concentration definition, water and preparation method before comparing source results.

Keep the comparison traceable

  1. Name the current salt, proposed sodium propionate grade and the reason for the change.
  2. Record the assay basis, material form and addition route, together with any formulation adjustment.
  3. Use a defined process, package and storage plan, and have the responsible technical team agree the observations and acceptance criteria.

BUYER QUESTIONS

Sodium propionate purchasing questions

Does the 10% solution pH predict the pH of my food?

No. It characterizes the material under a stated solution test. Measure the relevant food at defined stages and review the whole formulation.

What is needed to compare it with calcium propionate?

Identify both source grades, assay basis and incorporation method, state the reason for the sodium/calcium change, and define the food and storage observations.

Which records should support an order?

Match the proposed grade and source to a current specification and relevant TDS, COA and SDS. Request the moisture or drying method rather than relying on an unexplained limit.

Is the listed assay a sodium propionate or propionic acid result?

The supplied field names sodium propionate on a dry basis. Ask for the method and reporting calculation, and keep salt assay separate from results expressed as propionic acid.

Should I select a source because its solution pH is higher?

Not from that number alone. Compare results at the same defined concentration, temperature and preparation, then assess the complete food against your process and acceptance criteria.
