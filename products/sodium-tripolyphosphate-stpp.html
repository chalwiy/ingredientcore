PRODUCT OVERVIEW

What is sodium tripolyphosphate?

Sodium tripolyphosphate, also known as pentasodium triphosphate, is a sodium phosphate used to manage moisture and texture in foods such as processed meat and seafood. Grade, form and processing conditions affect how it performs.

Common names: Sodium Tripolyphosphate (STPP), Pentasodium Triphosphate, Pentasodium Tripolyphosphate and Sodium Triphosphate.

Material identity reference: FAO/JECFA — Pentasodium Triphosphate.

BUYER QUESTIONS

STPP purchasing questions

Is P₂O₅ content the same as STPP content?

STPP content is reported as Na₅P₃O₁₀. The P₂O₅ value expresses phosphate content on a different basis. Request the analytical method and calculation basis for each figure.

Which specification should I use for an order?

Use the current, agreed specification for the exact grade and source. It should state the test methods, reporting basis and acceptance criteria.

What should I specify when choosing powder or granules?

State whether the ingredient enters a dry blend or a prepared solution or brine. Include particle-size requirements where relevant, preparation temperature, mixing sequence and dissolution time, together with your food application.

Can STPP replace SHMP at the same addition level?

STPP is a triphosphate, and commercial SHMP is a glassy sodium polyphosphate. Check each grade’s phosphate reporting basis and intended function, then plan a trial comparing STPP and SHMP in your own formulation and process.

Can I request a free sample and certificates?

Yes. Tell sales what you plan to test and which certificates you need. We will confirm the sample arrangements and the product, source, certificate holder and scope covered by the records.

What information helps you prepare an STPP quotation?

Include the required grade or standard, powder or granule preference, food application, estimated order quantity, destination market and any documents you need.
