CMC, Xanthan & Carrageenan: Food Hydrocolloid Selection

Compare hydration, flow and gel behavior in the named food system, using viscosity and gel measurements with matching methods.

INGREDIENTCORE KNOWLEDGE CENTER

Content updated 2026-10-03

Choose the food behavior first, then compare the exact grade and test method. Sodium CMC (cellulose gum), xanthan gum and carrageenan address different texture questions; a viscosity label, mesh heading or gel-strength number alone cannot select a material. Match hydration, formulation and process conditions before evaluating samples. No equal-weight substitution is assumed.

01

Define the texture problem first

Start with an observable result: particles settling, a sauce pouring poorly, an uneven gel, liquid released from a gel, or protein aggregation. These are different problems. Specify what changes, when it appears and how you will compare it with the current system.

02

Compare methods before numbers

Material identity, commercial grade and finished-food behavior are separate records. Request complete methods rather than comparing labels alone.

Compare the method-defined material result first, then evaluate the food. Matching one viscosity reading does not establish matching hydration, suspension or gel texture.

Ask for a complete measurement record

  • Sample: exact grade, source, lot and current method revision.
  • Preparation: concentration and mass basis, water or solvent, salt composition, addition order, mixing and hydration time.
  • Measurement: temperature, instrument, spindle or geometry, speed or shear rate, units and reading time.
  • For gels: also state the recipe, ion system, heating, cooling and holding, plus probe and test rate.
  • Food evaluation: keep the actual food result separate from the material’s solution or model-gel test.
03

Separate carrageenan type from processing

Kappa and iota are gelling carrageenan types, with behavior linked to the ionic system; lambda is a thickening type without its own gel formation. Refined or semi-refined describes processing, not those functional types.

Request the proposed carrageenan type, process identity and composition, including any standardizing salts, sugars or other blend components. These technical categories identify comparison questions; they are not a list of confirmed available grades.

Technical type and process distinctions: FAO: A Guide to the Seaweed Industry, Carrageenan (2003)

04

Keep studies tied to their food systems

Two studies, two different systems

The milk-tea study screened stabilizers in a prepared black-tea, skim-milk and sugar system using defined laboratory measurements. It does not establish a universal gum ranking for drinks.

The soy study used a soy-protein/rice-bran-oil model emulsion with laboratory reagents, controlled acidity and homogenization. It is not an edible recipe or a result for a supplied IngredientCore grade.

Use these studies to identify variables and measurement questions. Do not copy their experimental levels, treatment schedules or blend ratios as commercial recommendations. Dairy, soy and oat bases should each be evaluated in their own formulation.

Original studies: Milk-tea stabilizer screening (2023) · CMC in a soy-protein model emulsion (2022)

05

Prepare and record the sample comparison

  • Identify the source, grade, composition and specification version for each sample.
  • Define water/minerals, pH, protein, salt, oil/solids and other gums.
  • Record powder dispersion separately from hydration: addition order, mixing, temperature and time.
  • Keep a current-system benchmark; record heating, cooling, homogenization and holding.
  • Record viscosity or gel measurements with the full method and time after processing.
  • Observe the relevant flow, settling, aggregation, liquid separation and sensory texture through the intended storage evaluation.

Request the current specification and methods, TDS, sample or lot COA, SDS and customer-required certificates. Sales confirms certificate applicability by product, source, holder and scope. Free samples are available; contact sales for the product, quantity and shipping arrangements.

How to use this guide

This article supports initial screening and discussion. For any proposed supply, confirm the product's current specification, source, food-category permission, use conditions and finished-product performance before commercial use.

Buyer questions

Can two equal viscosity values identify interchangeable grades?

No. Check whether the concentration, liquid, preparation and instrument conditions match. Then evaluate the named grade in the actual food. A single matched reading cannot establish equivalent hydration or finished-food stability.

Does a finer mesh designation mean better food performance?

Not by itself. A particle-size description needs the sieve standard and passing percentage or distribution. Record powder dispersion, hydration and the food result separately; a mesh label is not a viscosity or suspension specification.

Does “refined” identify kappa carrageenan?

No. Refinement describes processing, while kappa, iota and lambda describe functional types. Ask for both dimensions and the composition of any standardized material or blend.

What should I send if I have a texture problem but no chosen gum?

Describe the food, observed change, when it occurs and the processing and storage conditions. State how you assess pouring, settling, gel texture or liquid release. Sales can discuss candidate records and a free-sample evaluation plan without treating all gums in this guide as your product order.
