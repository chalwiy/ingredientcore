Specification, TDS and COA: three different questions

Match agreed limits, typical properties and sample/batch results to the same material, source, revision and methods.

INGREDIENTCORE KNOWLEDGE CENTER

Content updated 2026-09-30

A specification identifies proposed or agreed material limits. A Technical Data Sheet (TDS) explains context. A Certificate of Analysis (COA) reports an identified sample or batch. Reading them together is more useful than asking for an unspecified certificate.

01

Use each document for its actual question

If columns extend beyond the frame, scroll the table horizontally.

Document purposes and evidence boundaries
RecordUse it to checkDoes not establish
SpecificationIdentity/grade, limits, units, methods, revision and agreement statusThat every value was tested on every batch.
TDS (Technical Data Sheet)Description, typical properties and technical contextThat typical values are guaranteed limits.
COA (Certificate of Analysis)Sample/lot, tests, methods and reported resultsFuture batches or finished-food performance.
SDS (Safety Data Sheet)Material-specific hazards and handling informationFood-grade status or order conformity.

Technical basis: OSHA — Hazard Communication: Safety Data Sheets

02

Resolve identity and scope before values

Match the offer, specification and COA: exact material, form/blend, producer/packing source and grade. A shared abbreviation can conceal another form or method. Confirm whether a specification is a reference example, proposed source record or agreed order version.

Keep a cross-reference between source, document title/revision, sample/lot and material. An older stored document does not necessarily describe the current offer.

03

Investigate a numerical disagreement

  • Check analyte and basis: compound assay is not automatically a phosphorus expression.
  • Match units, dry/as-is basis if relevant, solution concentration, preparation and temperature.
  • For viscosity/gel results compare instrument/settings and the method’s recipe or ionic conditions.
  • Distinguish typical value, limit and individual result; ask what “conforms” means if no result is listed.
  • Clarify scope and method before deciding equivalent or nonconforming.
04

Certificates are separate evidence

A management-system certificate needs a named holder, site, scope, issuing body and validity review. ISO explains that external bodies perform certification; an ISO name nearby does not make every product certified.

Source/product declarations need their own identity and conditions. Ask about Halal, Kosher, allergens, GMO, origin or other records for the specific source; do not assume a complete package exists.

Technical basis: ISO — Certification

05

Connect sample review to receiving checks

A representative COA supports early review. For an order, agree source/grade/specification version, expected batch records and deviation handling. Separate document review from sampling/testing required by the buyer’s acceptance plan.

These are matters to agree for a purchase, not evidence of an audited IngredientCore receiving, traceability or change-control system.

06

Request version and sharing permission

  • Give product/grade, food application, market and review purpose.
  • Name relevant records and methods; ask if current applicable versions can be shared.
  • Resolve issuer/site/grade differences and missing units or methods.
  • Agree a suitable sharing route for restricted files; a hidden public link is not protection.
How to use this guide

This article supports initial screening and discussion. For any proposed supply, confirm the product's current specification, source, food-category permission, use conditions and finished-product performance before commercial use.

Sources and scope

Use identity references, technical explanations and food-system studies for their stated purposes. Match each reference to its stated material, food system and test conditions.

  • OSHA — Hazard Communication: Safety Data Sheets

    US hazard-communication context; an SDS is not a food-grade certificate or batch acceptance report.

  • ISO — Certification

    Certification and independent issuing bodies; not verification of any company’s certificate or its coverage.
