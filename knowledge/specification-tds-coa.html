Specification vs TDS vs COA: Food Ingredient Documents

Match agreed limits, typical properties and sample/batch results to the same material, source, revision and methods.

INGREDIENTCORE BUYING GUIDES

Content revised 2026-10-03

A specification states the proposed or agreed material criteria. A TDS gives technical context and typical properties. A COA reports an identified sample or lot. Match all three to the same grade, source, revision and methods; use an SDS separately for hazard and handling information.

01

Use each document for its actual question

Start with the decision you need to make: agree material limits, understand technical behavior, review a lot, or check handling information. Document titles and formats vary by source; read the fields and scope rather than relying on the filename.

Specification, TDS, COA and SDS: a purchasing comparison
DocumentWhat to reviewWhat it does not establish
SpecificationExact material/grade, acceptance limits or ranges, units, methods, basis and revision. Clarify whether it is proposed or agreed for the purchase.A result for a particular lot, or proof that every field is tested on every lot.
Technical Data Sheet (TDS)Material description, typical properties, preparation or application information for the named grade.That a typical value is a contractual acceptance limit.
Certificate of Analysis (COA)Identified sample or lot, applicable specification, reported results and methods or method references.The performance of future lots or of the ingredient in your finished food.
Safety Data Sheet (SDS)Material identity, hazard communication, handling and storage information for the relevant jurisdiction.Food-grade identity, market permission or batch conformity.

OSHA describes SDS content in the US hazard-communication context. Use that reference to understand handling information, separately from the purchasing specification and COA.

Reference: OSHA — Safety Data Sheets

02

Match identity, source and revision first

Check the product name, chemical or blend identity, hydration form, grade designation and proposed source before comparing values. Connect the quotation, specification, sample label and COA to the same material.

  • Record the document issuer, title, revision or issue date and the relevant lot/sample identifier.
  • Ask whether a COA is representative, for the sample you received, or for the shipment being offered.
  • If a document covers a family of grades or sites, identify which entry applies to the proposed material.

For Sodium Tripolyphosphate (STPP), the product page shows selected fields from supplied product information. Request a complete current specification for the proposed grade and source before using that extract as an order acceptance record.

If a COA says “conforms” instead of giving a number, ask how the conclusion was obtained and which criterion and method it refers to. Resolve missing units or conditions before judging two grades equivalent.

Use the Sodium CMC product guide and hydrocolloid selection guide to identify the viscosity and application questions relevant to your food.

04

Keep certificates and declarations separate

A COA reports material testing; a management-system certificate concerns a named organization, site and scope. A source declaration addresses a stated question, such as origin or an allergen requirement. They are different records.

ISO explains that external bodies perform certification; ISO itself does not issue certificates. For any certificate, review the holder, issuing body, covered site/activity, validity and relevance to the proposed supply.

Reference: ISO — Certification

IngredientCore can provide certificates for your purchasing review. Sales will confirm the applicable product, source, certificate holder and scope. Name any Halal, Kosher, allergen, GMO or origin requirements so the relevant records and availability can be discussed.

See product documents and certificate requests for the available request routes.

05

Connect the sample record to the order

Keep a record of the sample grade/source, document revisions and trial observations. A useful sample does not by itself define the material that will be ordered.

  • Agree the proposed commercial grade/source and specification version.
  • Specify which lot records are expected and how receiving checks will be performed.
  • Record the contact and agreed action for a missing document, deviation or proposed source/specification change.

The ingredient-source qualification guide explains how to combine these records with buyer approval and application evaluation. These are purchasing questions to agree, rather than a claim about an audited IngredientCore control system.

06

Send a precise document request

Include the product or grade, application, destination market and review purpose. Name the records and the fields or methods your team needs, instead of requesting an unspecified “certificate.”

Illustrative request — not a customer case

Please provide the current specification and TDS for the proposed food-grade STPP source, a representative COA identifying its sample or lot, the SDS relevant to our destination, and applicable certificates. Please identify document revisions, assay/reporting bases, pH test conditions, and certificate holder/site/scope.
