SAPP vs. SALP in bakery leavening systems

Separate acid–bicarbonate balance from gas-release timing, then compare named grades under the actual hold and baking schedule.

INGREDIENTCORE KNOWLEDGE CENTER

Content updated 2026-09-30

Compare SAPP with an exactly identified acidic SALP grade as parts of a complete chemical leavening system. The question is how the acid component and bicarbonate behave through mixing, holding and heating—not whether two names sound similar.

01

Resolve the material and grade

SAPP identity does not define a reaction-rate grade. The JECFA SALP reference here is specifically the acidic material; an unspecified sodium aluminum phosphate label needs clarification before a bakery comparison.

Request grade designation, source, particle information, reaction-test definition and neutralizing-value basis where supplied. A monograph is not a reaction certificate for the offered grade.

Technical basis: FAO JECFA — Disodium Pyrophosphate · FAO JECFA — Sodium Aluminium Phosphate, Acidic

02

Distinguish balance from timing

Neutralizing value describes acid capacity in relation to bicarbonate on a stated basis. Reaction rate describes when gas is generated under specified conditions. A rate code does not by itself balance acid and bicarbonate.

ICL’s technical paper explains balance and model dough reaction measurements for its materials. Its curves are method- and grade-specific: use them to understand what to ask for, not as curves for IngredientCore supply.

Technical basis: ICL — Leavening: It’s a Simple Equation (2025)

04

Avoid an unbalanced substitution test

Keep the current system unchanged as benchmark. Have the formulator review acid–bicarbonate balance before testing candidates. Record changes to bicarbonate or other ingredients explicitly; a balance difference must not be mistaken for a reaction-rate difference.

Keep flour lot, portioning and heating comparable. Compare fresh and held batter under the actual schedule. A waffle study examined leavening alongside fat and water variables; its findings concern that fresh-egg waffle system.

Technical basis: Original study — Waffle batter and leavening study (2017)

05

Request a testable grade description

  • Food and current leavening system, including other acid components.
  • Mix-to-bake timeline, temperature, chilling/freezing and bake profile.
  • Reaction method, model recipe, sampling times and grade-code definition.
  • Neutralizing-value definition/basis and current specification, if available.
  • Sample identity and its correspondence to the commercial source; intended food category and market.
06

Review the market separately

Baking trials inform lift, crumb and sensory decisions. They do not establish local permission, aluminum-related requirements or label wording. Check the exact material, food category and destination conditions without extending one market’s rule to another.

How to use this guide

This article supports initial screening and discussion. For any proposed supply, confirm the product's current specification, source, food-category permission, use conditions and finished-product performance before commercial use.

Sources and scope

Use identity references, technical explanations and food-system studies for their stated purposes. Match each reference to its stated material, food system and test conditions.

  • FAO JECFA — Disodium Pyrophosphate

    Identity and synonyms; a chemical entry does not define a reaction-rate grade.

  • FAO JECFA — Sodium Aluminium Phosphate, Acidic

    Acidic material identity in the cited monograph; not all materials called SALP or current local permission.

  • ICL — Leavening: It’s a Simple Equation (2025)

    Producer technical explanation of acid–bicarbonate balance and model dough reaction tests. Its grades and curves do not describe IngredientCore supply.

  • Original study — Waffle batter and leavening study (2017)

    A fresh-egg waffle system; supports recording batter/line variables, not a universal SAPP/SALP substitution rule.
