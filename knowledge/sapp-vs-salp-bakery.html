SAPP vs. SALP: Leavening Balance, Timing & Grade Selection

Separate acid–bicarbonate balance from gas-release timing, then compare named grades under the actual hold and baking schedule.

INGREDIENTCORE BUYING GUIDE

Content revised 2026-10-03

Compare a named SAPP grade with an exactly identified acidic SALP grade in the complete acid–bicarbonate system. Neutralizing value addresses acid capacity; reaction tests address gas-release timing. Neither a shared leavening role nor an equal-weight swap establishes equivalence.

Start with the exact material

The SAPP identity does not define a commercial reaction grade. The SALP reference used here concerns acidic sodium aluminum phosphate (also spelled aluminium). Basic SALP is outside this leavening comparison; an unspecified SALP name is not enough. Match the exact acidic composition, hydration state and any blend components to the proposed sample.

Identity references do not establish an offered grade, neutralizing value or food-market permission.

Separate acid capacity from reaction timing

Neutralizing value is conventionally expressed as the mass of sodium bicarbonate neutralized by a stated mass of leavening acid. Confirm the supplier’s basis and method. Reaction timing describes gas generation under specified mixing, holding and heating conditions.

A reaction-grade number is not a neutralizing value. Request both definitions rather than converting a grade code into an acid–bicarbonate balance.

ICL’s producer paper explains this distinction with its own materials and model dough tests. Those grades, curves and values are not IngredientCore specifications.

Design the comparison before replacing ingredients

Keep the existing system as the benchmark. Have the formulator review acid capacity and bicarbonate balance and record recipe changes before comparing reaction timing. Match flour, mixing, portions and heating, and evaluate fresh and held batter.

The published fresh-egg waffle study used a specified SAPP40 material and examined leavening agents, fat sources and water. It did not test SALP. Its pilot-scale waffle findings support recording the recipe and process, not a universal SAPP-to-SALP replacement rule.

Request a grade description you can evaluate

  • Exact identity, form, source and commercial grade.
  • Current specification and available TDS or COA for that material.
  • Neutralizing-value definition, basis and method.
  • Reaction-test recipe, temperature, sampling times and grade-code definition.
  • Correspondence between the sample and the proposed commercial material.

Include the current acid and bicarbonate system, hold time and baking profile when asking for a sample.

Review destination requirements separately

Baking trials answer formulation questions. They do not establish regulatory permission or label wording. Review the exact food category, material and destination requirements; do not extend one market’s rule to another.

No fixed dose, equal-weight exchange or aluminum-related limit is recommended in this guide.

Buyer questions

Does a SAPP reaction-grade code tell me the neutralizing value?

No. Ask for the grade-code definition and reaction method separately from the neutralizing-value basis. The commercial code cannot be read as chemical purity, pH or acid capacity.

Can an equivalent acid balance guarantee an equivalent baked result?

No. The formulator must also compare gas-release timing, the hold schedule and the way the food sets during heating. Record finished pH, shape, crumb and sensory observations against the current system.

What should a held-batter sample request include?

Include the current acid–bicarbonate system, flour, mixing temperature, waiting interval, chilling or freezing and baking schedule. Request a named acidic grade, current specification, relevant TDS, representative COA and capacity/reaction methods. Link the sample to the commercial source being reviewed.

Sources and scope

Use identity references, technical explanations and food-system studies for their stated purposes. Match each reference to its stated material, food system and test conditions.

  • FAO JECFA — Disodium Pyrophosphate

    Identity and synonyms; a chemical entry does not define a reaction-rate grade.

  • FAO JECFA — Sodium Aluminium Phosphate, Acidic

    Acidic material identity in the cited monograph; not all materials called SALP or current local permission.

  • ICL — Leavening: It’s a Simple Equation (2025)

    Producer technical explanation of acid–bicarbonate balance and model dough reaction tests. Its grades and curves do not describe IngredientCore supply.

  • Original study — Waffle batter and leavening study (2017)

    Full text read: a specified SAPP40 fresh-egg waffle system with no SALP test arm; not a general substitution rule.
