How to Write a Food Ingredient Inquiry: Quote, Sample or Documents

Give the food, observed problem, process, evaluation method, grade requirements and purchasing context in one readable inquiry.

INGREDIENTCORE BUYING GUIDES

Content revised 2026-10-03

Start with the decision you need: a quotation, free sample, documents or application discussion. Name the ingredient or food problem, relevant grade and test conditions, destination and requested next step. A short brief with specific observations is more useful than an unsupported performance target.

01

Choose the decision and request type

State whether you need a quotation, free sample, product documents or application discussion. If you are still selecting an ingredient, name the food and observed problem instead of guessing a grade.

No complete recipe is needed for an initial request. Provide the information relevant to the decision, then agree how any detailed or restricted files will be shared.

02

Describe the food and observation

Replace “better quality” with the specific observation: loss of lift after holding, sediment in a drink, liquid released from a gel, or a defined texture change after thawing. Give the current material or system if known.

The application directory expands these questions by food. Preservation and safety evaluations belong with the responsible qualified team; an inquiry does not validate a process.

03

Attach conditions to specification targets

Provide the exact material form or grade and the important specification fields. A target number needs its unit, analytical basis and test conditions to be useful.

  • For a phosphate assay, say which substance or equivalent is reported and identify the analytical basis.
  • For CMC viscosity, include concentration, preparation, temperature, instrument and settings if known.
  • For a carrageenan gel or leavening reaction target, identify the method and relevant preparation or model conditions.

If you do not have the method, ask for it. The product-document guide explains how to separate a typical property, acceptance limit and lot result.

For the bakery example below, ICL’s technical paper distinguishes acid–bicarbonate balance from grade-specific reaction behavior. Use that distinction to frame questions, without copying its formulations, curves or quantities.

Reference: ICL — Leavening technical paper (2025)

04

Use a short, complete inquiry

Illustrative bakery brief — not a customer case or formula

We are reviewing a leavening acid for a batter held before baking. The issue is reduced lift after holding compared with fresh batter. Please discuss an identified SAPP or acidic SALP grade and its available specification, neutralizing-value basis and reaction-test information. We can share our current system and mixing, hold and bake conditions by email.

Please confirm the proposed grade/source, representative COA and applicable certificates. We would like a free evaluation sample and a quotation for our intended quantity and destination. Please discuss sample quantity, shipping and commercial arrangements with us.

Add your measured conditions, estimated quantity and destination before sending. Use the SAPP versus acidic SALP guide for the technical comparison; this example does not select a grade or prescribe a replacement.
