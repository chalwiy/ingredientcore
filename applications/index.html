01 / FOOD APPLICATION

Meat, poultry & seafood

For cooked meat, poultry, formed products, fish or shrimp, separate the amount taken up during treatment from water retained after draining, cooking or thawing.

The buyer’s question

Describe cooking loss, thaw drip, texture variation or brine preparation. Name the species and product form before comparing phosphates.

Conditions that affect selection

  • Species or protein source, whole-muscle / formed product and prior freezing history.
  • Salt, starting pH, water/minerals, mixing or injection/tumbling, treatment time and temperature.
  • Draining, cooking, freezing and thawing stages; use the same weighing basis for comparisons.

What to observe in a sample trial

  • Record treatment uptake separately from drained, cooked or thawed mass loss.
  • Compare texture, appearance, taste and any brine dissolution issue with the current formulation.
  • Identify the grade and source used in each trial; repeat the agreed measurements.

Bakery: leavening & mold control

Cakes, muffins, waffles, baking mixes and yeast-raised bread present different questions. Chemical leavening controls gas generation; preservation review addresses post-bake spoilage.

The buyer’s question

Is the issue lift after holding, uneven crumb, proof behavior or mold during storage? Keep leavening and preservation trials separate.

Conditions that affect selection

  • For leavening: acid and bicarbonate balance, flour / solids, water addition and mixing.
  • Mix-to-bake hold, chilling or freezing, portion size and heating schedule.
  • For preservation: pH, water activity if known, cooling, hygiene, pack and storage conditions.

What to observe in a sample trial

  • Compare lift, shape, crumb, finished-food pH and taste after the actual holding schedule.
  • For yeast dough, record proof and baked texture when comparing preservation systems.
  • Define a qualified shelf-life study separately from texture trials; monitor sensory changes as well as the agreed spoilage endpoints.

03 / FOOD APPLICATION

Dairy & dairy alternatives

Milk drinks, desserts, processed cheese and plant-based alternatives need different briefs. Name the protein or plant base and decide whether the product should pour, remain dispersed or form a gel.

The buyer’s question

Describe sediment, visible protein aggregates, liquid release, unwanted gelation or cheese texture. A milk-based result does not establish performance in soy, oat or pea systems.

04 / FOOD APPLICATION

Beverages

For fruit-style drinks, particulate beverages, coffee/tea drinks and protein drinks, identify what settles or separates. A thicker drink is not necessarily a more stable one.

The buyer’s question

Distinguish particle settling, protein aggregates, an oil ring and a spoilage concern. Describe when the defect appears: after hydration, heating, filling or storage.

  • pH, acid system, water/minerals, dissolved solids, particle properties and protein source.

  • Package, storage temperature and duration; any existing preservation and hygiene controls.

05 / FOOD APPLICATION

Sauces, dressings & prepared foods

Pourable dressings, cooked sauces and prepared fillings differ in oil, starch, protein and storage conditions. Start with the required flow and the separation you observe.

The buyer’s question

Should the food pour, cling or suspend particles? Distinguish sediment and liquid release from oil/water separation before selecting a thickener.

Conditions that affect selection

  • Oil/water balance, emulsifying ingredients, starch, protein, solids, salts and pH.
  • Dispersion, hydration, shear, cooking, cooling, filling and any reheating.
  • Intended pack and chilled, ambient or frozen storage; freeze/thaw route if relevant.

What to observe in a sample trial

  • Compare pouring, cling, particle settling and sensory texture at a stated temperature.
  • Record oil separation and liquid release separately through storage and any reheating or freeze/thaw.
  • Agree preservation study criteria separately from flow or separation measurements.

Products to review

Fermentation nutrient systems

Diammonium phosphate (DAP) is listed as yeast food in the cited JECFA monograph. MAP/DAP provide ammonium and phosphate; MKP provides potassium and phosphate, so they change the medium differently.

Products to review

Process variables

Organism and food-processing route, complete nutrient medium, existing nitrogen/phosphate/potassium inputs and pH control.

Sample evaluation

Track the process response and finished-product criteria selected by the qualified process team; record both favorable and unfavorable changes.

Records to request

Ask for exact identity, grade specification, composition basis, relevant impurity methods and source-specific COA/handling records.

Calcium phosphate powders & premixes
